From messy CRM data to the next best action.

    How prioritisation works
    1. Check account status. Respect recorded holds and restrictions. Identify existing customers, open opportunities and unresolved CRM information.
    2. Respect timing. Keep future callbacks, deferrals and recent unanswered contact waiting.
    3. Identify the next task. Distinguish due callbacks, recent replies, qualification and missing-information checks.
    4. Estimate commercial value. Use annual paid tickets and ticket prices to calculate illustrative gross fees.
    5. Order comparable work. Within the same action category and timing, prioritise higher estimated fees, then easier switching. Unknown fees follow known values.
    6. Explain the recommendation. Show the next action and its reason for human review. Missing information becomes a check for a person; the tool does not research the web.
    Calculations and tie-breaks
    • Annual paid tickets = paid events per year x paid tickets per event.
    • Annual GMV = annual paid tickets x average ticket price.
    • Illustrative gross fees = annual paid tickets x (average ticket price x fee percentage / 100 + fee per ticket).
    • Upload a CSV to see the fee assumptions used for that run.
    • Due commitments come first, then recent replies, qualification, missing-information checks, CRM checks, policy review, waiting and existing relationships. Restrictions block acquisition work.
    • Public activity evidence, unresolved checks and account ID resolve remaining ties. Missing fee inputs stay Unknown; GMV never substitutes for fees in ranking.
    • Fee rates are illustrative assumptions. Gross fees are not profit or a revenue forecast; processing costs are not deducted. GMV bands are descriptive and do not determine ranking.
